Self-Portrait with Cropped Hair

Frida Kahlo, 1940

So, Frida Kahlo gets divorced. Then she cuts off her hair. Look at the floor. It's not just a trim. It's everywhere. Around the chair legs. Out to the edges. One piece hangs off the chair back like it's trying to climb up and reattach. And... she's still holding some. She's got the scissors in one hand. A lock of hair in the other. This is the breakup haircut. In a movie, this is where the music comes up. Ya know, new hair, new clothes. Out walks a whole new person. Here, she hasn't even put the scissors down. So... she and Diego Rivera have been married ten years. Affairs on both sides. By late 1939, they're divorced. The next year, she paints this. Now look at the clothes. A man's suit. Way too big. Possibly his. No flowers. No long dress. None of those braids piled up on her head. That whole look Diego loved. She's still wearing an earring, though. And those little heeled shoes under the big trousers. And there's music. She paints the soundtrack right across the top. Words and notes from a Mexican song. "Look, if I loved you, it was for your hair. Now that you're bald, I don't love you anymore." Hell of a song to hang over your own head. Then try reading her face. Not much help. The hair makes more of a fuss than she does. She cuts it off. Then sits down and paints it all back into the picture. Every strand and tangle. Thirteen months after the divorce, she marries Diego again. On his birthday. But look back down here. The scissors are still in her hand. And nobody's swept the floor.
