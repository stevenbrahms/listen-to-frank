Self-Portrait with Two Flowers in Her Raised Left Hand

Paula Modersohn-Becker, 1907

She’s looking right at you. Kinda like she’s the one sizing you up. That’s Paula Modersohn-Becker. She painted this herself. A year earlier, she’s in Paris, living apart from her husband. Painting herself naked. One of the first modern women artists to do that. In one of those pictures, she gives herself a round belly, as if she’s pregnant. She isn’t. Here, she is. Look at the hand along the bottom. Resting over her belly. And in the other hand, two flowers. A dark red one and a pink one, held right up beside her face. Now get close to that face. The paint’s thick. Ridges across her forehead. Pink around the eyes. That big pink ear. She’s really pushing the stuff around. And there’s another flower. Scratched into her cheek. Probably with the end of her brush. So she’s holding two, and she’s worked another right into her face. She’d been studying Egyptian mummy portraits at the Louvre. Faces painted on wood, placed over the dead. Nearly two thousand years old, some of them. And still, there’s somebody looking out. You can see the connection. The big eyes. The solid, almost masklike face. But hers has all this rough paint sitting on it. You can follow where her hand’s been. She paints this in 1907. That November, she has a daughter. They keep her in bed for eighteen days. That’s what doctors did back then. Then she’s finally allowed up. She combs her hair. Puts in some red roses people brought her. And she walks, slowly, into the living room, where the baby’s in her crib. She picks her up. And suddenly falls to the floor. A blood clot. She’s thirty-one. She made more than seven hundred paintings. Her husband said her last words were “Wie schade.” What a pity.
