Painting, 4

Vasudeo S. Gaitonde, 1962

Okay, so he wouldn’t even give it a name. Painting Four. That’s all you get. Most of his paintings, he just left untitled. And he didn’t like “abstract,” either. Vasudeo Gaitonde. Bombay guy. Came up with the painters building modern Indian art after independence, then pretty much went off on his own. Non-objective. That’s what he called it. He wasn’t looking at a tree and making it blurry. There was just no tree to begin with. So have a look. All that olive green, with this dark gray band running across the middle. Almost, like evening’s coming in. Out in the gray, on both sides, these black scratchy marks. Like something on stilts at low tide. A few little bits of yellow and orange in them. Like a couple of windows left on. Then right above the middle, a block of gold. Right below it, a slab of white. He’d use a roller and a painting knife, putting layers on, scraping some back off. You can see the scraped bits up top. He said painting started with silence. And this guy could wait. Months sometimes, before he put any paint down. Five or six paintings a year. This one goes to a New York group show, and straight from the show to MoMA. Then a Rockefeller grant gets him a year in the city. Apparently, the guy running the grant wasn’t too happy. Gaitonde’s spending all his time at the movies. Anyway, twelve years after he dies, one of his sells for more than any Indian painter had ever brought at auction. It was called Untitled… naturally. He wasn’t much of a talker, either. They made a whole documentary about him, and he doesn’t say a single word in it. When MoMA asks him about his work, he writes back that it’s the reflection of nature. And then: “I want to say things in few words.”
