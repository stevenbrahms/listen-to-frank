Tableau No. 2 / Composition No. V

Piet Mondrian, 1914 · Gallery 503 · Floor 5

Alright, try following one of those black lines all the way across, through all that pink and dusty yellow. Good luck with that. A critic back then called it a map. Or a… labyrinth. So… get a little closer. Just left of center, there’s a tiny cross. And above the middle, there’s this block with a rounded top. Like an arch. Nobody said church. But that’s probably where this started. Notre-Dame-des-Champs, a neighborhood church in Paris. When Mondrian moves to Paris, he even drops a letter from his name. Check the signature, bottom left. Back home it was Mondriaan, with two a’s. Paris gets one. So… Picasso’s in the same neighborhood, taking guitars apart. And Mondrian… he wants to see how far you can take that. Far enough, it turns out, to get to those grid paintings he’s famous for. That summer, he heads home to Holland for a few weeks. His father’s sick. And in The Hague, he gets his first show of his own. A Rotterdam paper picks up on the new spelling. Then says the paintings look like the floor plan of a pig market. A preacher from Gouda buys three. This is one of them. Then, the week the show closes, the First World War starts. And everybody says it’ll be over soon, so he stays. Every month, he sends rent for a Paris studio he can’t get to. His paints are still in there. So he’s drawing the North Sea. Same stop-and-start black strokes. The water gets the horizontals, the pier gets the verticals. Now look back at the corners of this one. The middle’s packed, but out there, it thins out to bare gray. Never makes it to the edge. The visit lasts five years.
