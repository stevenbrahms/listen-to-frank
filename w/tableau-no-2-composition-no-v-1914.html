Tableau No. 2 / Composition No. V

Piet Mondrian, 1914 · Gallery 503 · Floor 5

Alright, try following one of those black lines all the way across, through all that pink and dusty yellow. Good luck. Now look a little up and left of center. That tiny cross. And above the middle, there's a shape with a rounded top. Kinda like an arch. This might have started with a church. Notre-Dame-des-Champs. Near Mondrian's studio in Paris. But there's not much church left. Picasso's in the same neighborhood, taking guitars apart. And Mondrian's seeing how much of the thing you can lose. Eventually, he gets to those grids he's famous for. Even his name gets shorter. Look at the signature, bottom left. Back home, it was Mondriaan. Two a's. Paris just gets one. And look at the corners. Everything's crowded into the middle, and out toward the edges, the lines start giving out. Just gray. It's 1914. That summer, he goes home to Holland for a couple of weeks. Meanwhile, a gallery in The Hague's got a whole show of his new Paris paintings up. He gets there for the last week. A Rotterdam paper notices the new spelling. Then says the paintings look like the floor plan of a pig market. He's hoping to sell one. Enough for the rent. A preacher from Gouda buys three. This is one of them. Then, the week the show closes, the First World War starts. Mondrian wants to go back. But that means the long way round, through England or Germany. Which is expensive. And everybody figures it'll be over soon. So he stays. His studio's in Paris. His paints too. In Holland, he starts making drawings of the North Sea. With the pier sticking out into it. And he's doing to that view what he was doing to buildings back in Paris. Breaking it down. Same short, stop-and-start marks. Horizontals for the water. Verticals for the pier. He's gone from a church to the sea. And he keeps finding these lines. Meanwhile, he's still paying rent on that Paris studio. He was just coming home for a couple of weeks. The visit lasts five years.
