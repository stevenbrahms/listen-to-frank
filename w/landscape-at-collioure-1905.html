Landscape at Collioure

Henri Matisse, 1905 · Gallery 506 · Floor 5

You can feel the sun coming off this before you can tell what it is. There’s a hillside in there. Some trees. So, Matisse started out as a law clerk. Then appendicitis puts him in bed. His mom brings him a box of paints. That’s more or less the end of the legal career. Years later, he’s spending the summer in Collioure. Little fishing town near the Spanish border. His friend André Derain’s there too. And Matisse is making the ground pink. See that strip coming up the middle? It’s a path. Follow it between the trees. Over on the right, the trunks are red. That coral color up top is probably the hill. And between all those dabs? Bare canvas. A lot of the light’s coming from places he hasn’t painted. Looks like he might come back and fill them in. He’s done. The trees are the wrong color. But the heat feels right. Back in Paris, he’s working on a harbor for the autumn show. Little dots. One after another. He complains about how long it’s taking. That painting doesn’t make the show. The wild stuff from the summer does. Him and his friends, all in one room. And in the middle, a tidy little marble bust of a child. A critic takes a look. The bust. The paintings around it. “Donatello among the wild beasts.” Now, the bust is actually by Albert Marque. But he gets to be Donatello for the afternoon. Matisse and his friends are the beasts. Fauves. That sticks. Fauvism. Often called the first art movement of the century. Matisse later tells his son-in-law he still has no idea what the word means.
