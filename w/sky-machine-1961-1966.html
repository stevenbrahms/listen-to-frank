Sky Machine

Yoko Ono, 1961/1966 · Gallery 405 · Floor 4

This is a vending machine. An actual vending machine. A coin goes in, and out comes a little card. About the size of your thumb. It says sky. So you can buy some sky. Look at it. Brushed steel. No logo. Six little levers and a coin slot up top. Looks like it belongs in a bus station. Or a laundromat. It’s by Yoko Ono. Early sixties, New York. She’s making art out of instructions. Basically a sentence, and you just go do it yourself. This one, you can figure out. You know what to do with a coin. So, she has the idea, and then about five years later, she’s got the machine. And she wants it out on the street. Now, every card is handwritten. In pencil. Her initials on the back. So somebody’s sitting there writing sky, over and over, so a machine can sell it. The one thing nobody can own. And you’d walk off with a card in your pocket that says otherwise. During the war, Ono and her little brother and sister get sent out of Tokyo, into the countryside. Somewhere safer. She’s about twelve. She said there weren’t many beautiful things in her life back then. Except the sky.
