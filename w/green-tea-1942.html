Green Tea

Leonora Carrington, 1942 · Gallery 522 · Floor 5

So Leonora Carrington’s being presented at the royal court in London. Her family’s hoping this ends in a good marriage. Meanwhile, Leonora’s just trying to get through the ceremony. She’s brought a novel. And when she does choose somebody… it’s Max Ernst. By twenty, she’s off to France with him. Famous Surrealist, twenty-six years older and already married. Probably not who the family had in mind. Then the war comes. Ernst is arrested, and she flees to Spain, where she has a breakdown. Her family has her committed against her will to a mental hospital in Santander. They give her Cardiazol, a drug that causes seizures. That’s the treatment. She’s only twenty-three. The story goes that her family sends her old nanny to fetch her, by submarine. Seems like a stretch… but let’s just go with it. Next stop’s another institution in South Africa, but in Lisbon she gives her nurse the slip and walks into the Mexican embassy. In her telling, the ambassador assures her she’s safe. Not even the English can touch her. Then Renato Leduc, a poet and diplomat she knows from Paris, suggests they marry to get her out. So they hitch, and catch a ship to New York. There, she paints this. Look at those clipped hedges, the fountain, that carefully kept lawn. And out front, a woman wrapped in something like cowhide, her arms pinned and her eyes closed. Some people see that as Carrington herself. Farther back, more figures are wrapped in white. Underneath the lawn, there’s bats, eggs, something bundled like a body. And those two animals, a horse and something like a dog. Each has a tree growing out of its tail, chained to the other one’s tree. That same year, she writes on the back of an old painting from Santander: “For you, Renato, in the name of magic, the green which is my color.”
