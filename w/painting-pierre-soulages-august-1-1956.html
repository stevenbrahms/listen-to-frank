Painting

Pierre Soulages, August 1, 1956

Before he's the French painter famous for black, Pierre Soulages is a kid, drawing black lines on white paper. A friend of his sister's asks him what he's making. "A snow scene," he says. "With black ink." Years later, he thinks he understands what he was doing. Putting black next to the white made the paper look brighter. More like snow. Now look toward the lower left of this one. Those little streaks of olive and gold. Against this much black, a little goes a long way. And those big black strokes overlap almost like boards in a pile. Get closer. Thin lines of light along the edges, where the paint ridges up. Enough to keep the whole thing from becoming one solid lump. He makes his own tools. He's even used old shoe soles. Spread the paint. Drag it. Scrape some back. This one's called Painting. August 1, 1956. Now jump ahead. He's in the studio, working on another canvas. Black everywhere. Hours of work, and he thinks the thing's going badly. He's exhausted. Can't figure out why he's still working on a painting he doesn't like. So he goes to sleep for a while. Comes back. Same painting. But now he notices something. Light catching the black surface. Where the paint's smooth, one effect. Where it's ridged, another. He realizes he can paint with that. The light reflected off the black. Then a dealer asked him to come look at one of his old paintings. From the fifties. Soulages is worried. Something wrong with it? Nope. So he looks. But he still can't see what the fuss is about. So the dealer holds up his hands. Makes a little frame around one part. And there it is. A light effect he recognizes from his new work. Already there, all those years before. Somebody had to show him. Now come back to this one. Those thin lines along the edges. Where the black catches the light. And those little streaks of gold. He's given them a whole lot of black to shine against.
