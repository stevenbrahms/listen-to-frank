Dec. 17, 1979

On Kawara, 1979 · Gallery 413 · Floor 4

December 17, 1979. That’s it. That’s the painting. And the guy who made it won’t let you take his picture. Hardly gives interviews. But for years, his friends get these telegrams. Same thing every time. Just… I AM STILL ALIVE. His name’s On Kawara. Came over from Japan, settled in New York. And he’s one of the first people making art out of… basically, paperwork. A date. What time he got up. A telegram. Now, he sets himself a rule with these paintings. He’s got to finish each one on the day it says. By midnight. If he misses that, he destroys it. And these take a while. He puts on coat after coat, sands each one down, then paints the letters by hand. Look at them. White. Dead center. He does this for almost fifty years. Thousands of paintings, wherever he happens to be. He makes each one its own cardboard box. And often, he lines it with a clipping from that day’s paper. So the news is in there. Just not on the painting. Plenty have a one-line note, too. One day, it’s Monopoly with friends and a lot of spaghetti. Another day, the moon landing. And early on, one just says he’s afraid of these paintings. So what were you doing on December 17, 1979? It was a Monday. Nobody remembers a Monday. He painted this one. Then on Tuesday, he did it again. And when he dies, his official biography is one number. How many days he was alive. 29,771.
