Attica Series Desk

Cameron Rowland, 2016

You know this desk. Black steel. Fake-wood top. You may have seen it at a school office or a waiting room. But you've probably never wondered who made it. In 2025, Frieze magazine makes a list of the best artworks of the century so far. Puts this at number one. It was made by prisoners at the Attica Correctional Facility. Sold through Corcraft, the sales arm of New York's prison system. In the catalog, it's called the Attica Series. That's Corcraft's name for it. The same Attica where, in 1971, nearly thirteen hundred prisoners take control of the yard. A twenty-one-year-old named L. D. Barkley reads their statement to the news cameras. "We are men. We are not beasts." Among their demands, they want the state minimum wage for the work they do inside. And on their list, in capital letters: "STOP SLAVE LABOR." Four days later, the state retakes the prison. Helicopters. Tear gas. Gunfire. Thirty-nine people die in the retaking. Prisoners and hostages. All killed by gunfire from the state's forces. Barkley is one of them. He was just days away from getting out. So now it's 2016. An artist named Cameron Rowland gets this desk, changes nothing, and puts it in a show at Artists Space in New York. In the exhibition text, Rowland gives the pay range for people working in New York prisons. Ten cents to a dollar fourteen an hour. And getting the desk into the gallery takes some paperwork. Corcraft won't sell to an individual, but it does sell to certain nonprofits. And the gallery somehow qualifies. So the gallery registers as a customer. Its customer number becomes the name of the show. Later, the desk comes here to MoMA. But Rowland won't sell it. MoMA has to rent it. Five years at a time. And that whole five-year rental costs what the desk originally cost in the prison catalog. MoMA can show it. Has to look after it. Can't buy it. Now, the essay Rowland wrote for that first gallery show, it opens with the Thirteenth Amendment. The one that abolished slavery. There's an exception in it, which states: "Except as a punishment for crime."
