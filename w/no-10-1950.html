NO. 10

MARK ROTHKO, 1950

Look around. Rothko has somehow taken over the whole room. Most artists in this building get a wall. Maybe a corner. This guy gets the place to himself. No, he didn’t call MoMA and demand it. But this is pretty close to how he wanted these paintings seen. Rothko was a nightmare about installation. The paintings had to hang low. The light had to be dim. He didn’t want you strolling past, glancing over, checking Rothko off the list. He wanted you stuck in here with them. Which is exactly where you are. So use it. Get closer than feels polite. About eighteen inches. That was his number. Close enough that the yellow stops feeling like a color on a wall and starts becoming the air in front of your face. And watch the edges. The blue pressing into the yellow. The gray underneath swallowing whatever sits above it. Nothing here gets to mind its own business. Now, the funny part. This was the first Rothko ever to enter MoMA’s collection. In 1952, the architect Philip Johnson bought it from the Betty Parsons Gallery and gave it to the museum. And in curator Dorothy Miller’s notes, beside this painting, she wrote: “Number 10. MMA?” Question mark. Fair enough. Seven and a half feet of rectangles in 1952. You can see why there was a question mark. They took the chance. Now he gets the room.
