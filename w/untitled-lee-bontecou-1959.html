Untitled

Lee Bontecou, 1959 · Gallery 408 · Floor 4

Look at the hole. Not the whole thing. The hole. That’s not paint. It’s an actual cavity, lined with black cloth. You can’t see the bottom. Follow the panels in. They all funnel toward that hole. So where does a black like that come from? Apparently… Rome. Lee Bontecou’s there on a scholarship, working with a welding torch. She turns the oxygen down, and the flame goes cool. Starts pouring out soot. In that soot, she said she’d found a real black. The kind you could sink into. Anyway, that hole cheats. It’s black cloth. Some of that soot’s out here too, in those smudges. So, back in New York, she lives over this laundry. And they keep throwing out these old conveyor belts. Just filthy canvas. So she welds a steel frame, stretches the belts across it, and wires every piece down. See those little whiskers along the seams? That’s the wire. This is one of her first. Sputnik’s gone up. She said nobody knew a thing about black holes back then. Just huge, dangerous things out there you couldn’t touch. And she’s thrilled. She calls them all Untitled. Nobody gets to boss you around about what you’re seeing. Including her. These make her famous fast. She’s showing alongside Jasper Johns and Rauschenberg. One of very few women. Basically a boys’ club. Then she just walks away. She teaches in Brooklyn so she doesn’t have to depend on a gallery. Works in a barn in Pennsylvania. For about thirty years, almost nobody sees a thing. Then there’s a big museum show. She’s in her seventies. Turns out she’s been making work the whole time.
