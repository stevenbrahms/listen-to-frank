Dance (I)

Henri Matisse, 1909 · Gallery 506 · Floor 5

So, Matisse painted this in less than a week. Nearly thirteen feet across. Five pink naked people dancing on a hill. And he’s still working out the poses while he paints. Get close. The paint’s so thin you can see the weave of the canvas through it. Those faint grey lines beside the arms and legs are places where a body used to be. He changed his mind. Down on the left, where the blue meets the hill, the paint’s run. He leaves that too. Now follow the dancer stretched across the bottom. Her whole body’s reaching for the woman on the left, trying to keep the circle together. Look at the hand she’s reaching for. Barely a hand. More of a smudge. That’s not much of a grip. Blue sky, green hill, five bodies. Hardly a face between them. But you can feel what they’re doing. Nobody had really stripped a painting down this far before. Matisse figured the camera could take care of the details. It was better at them anyway. Painting could get on with the feeling. So where does the dance come from? Not from the famous dancer down the street. He watched her, and his daughter says he found her a bit limited. He pointed to a dance hall in Montmartre instead. Soldiers and workmen going round with their girlfriends. Then a collector from Moscow, Sergei Shchukin, sees this painting. He takes Matisse to lunch, and they work out an order for his staircase. Which makes this enormous thing the sketch. Matisse paints another version. Red bodies this time. That one goes to Russia. This one he keeps. Twenty-seven years. It’s just rolled up in his studio. Like a rug.
