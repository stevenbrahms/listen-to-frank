Dance (I)

Henri Matisse, 1909 · Gallery 506 · Floor 5

Matisse paints this in less than a week. It’s almost thirteen feet wide. Five pink naked people dancing on a hill. And he’s still working out the poses as he goes. Get up close. The paint’s so thin you can see the weave of the canvas. Those faint gray lines beside the arms and legs? Places where a body used to be. He changed his mind. Down on the left, where the blue meets the hill, the paint’s run. He leaves that too. Now follow the dancer stretched across the bottom. Her whole body’s reaching for the woman on the left. Looks like she’s trying to keep the circle together. And look at the hand she’s reaching for. Barely a hand. More of a smudge. Blue sky, green hill, five bodies. Hardly a face between them. But you can feel what they’re doing. This is the painting where Matisse figures the camera can handle the details. A hundred times better and more quickly, he said. Painting could get on with the feeling. Nobody had taken this much out of a picture before. So where does the dance come from? Matisse pointed to a dance hall in Montmartre. Soldiers and workmen going round with their girlfriends. Now a collector from Moscow wants something big for his staircase. Sergei Shchukin. He sees this before there’s any color on it. Takes Matisse to lunch, and they work out the order. Then he writes with a request. He’s got three little girls living in the house. Says he can’t hang nudes on the staircase. Could Matisse do “the same dance, but with girls in dresses”? Then another letter. He’s decided he’ll brave public opinion. The dancers can stay naked. And this enormous thing is just the sketch. Matisse paints another version. Red bodies this time. That one goes to Russia. This one he keeps for twenty-seven years. And for years, it’s rolled up in his studio. Like a rug.
