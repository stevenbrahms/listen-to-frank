Braun SK4 Record Player

Dieter Rams & Hans Gugelot, 1956

In 1956, a record player was furniture. A big wooden cabinet with the machine shut away inside it, like something you shouldn't look at. Then Braun put out this. A pale metal box, wooden sides, and a lid you can see straight through. Hans Gugelot and Dieter Rams designed it, back when everything electric was trying to look expensive. Big dials. Heavy cabinets. Weighed a ton, so you'd know it was serious. They went the other way. Buttons that look like buttons. Knobs that behave like knobs. Nothing showing off. Rams spent the rest of his career turning that into rules. Good design is honest. Good design is unobtrusive. Good design is as little design as possible. Half the things in your pocket still run on those sentences. Now. The lid. The first plan was sheet metal. Then the engineers switched the speaker on. The lid rattled. So they swapped it for plastic. Gugelot thought the stuff was a fad. The name that stuck was Snow White's Coffin. Two years later, MoMA took one. The coffin outlived the furniture.
