Untitled (Fragment 5)

Bridget Riley, 1965 · Gallery 410 · Floor 4

Okay, so, get right up close to this one. Look for the brushstrokes. Nothin'. No canvas either. No paint. Ink on clear plastic. Alright, so back up a little. Those black bands go up and over, like a rainbow. Then drop into two big bellies, with a little bump between them. Kind of a mustache. Follow them out to the ends. The stripes squeeze down to almost nothing. Then curl under. Bridget Riley made this. She's a painter from London. One of the big names in Op art. Pictures that mess with your eyes. You stand still. The thing shivers anyway. Her paintings begin with studies. Lots of them. But she sets seven aside. These won't become paintings. She wants them to "remain as fragments of a theme." So she makes prints. Her first serious go at printmaking, and she decides to skip paper. Straight to plastic. "We all wanted the new!" she said. There's just one problem. The fancy print shop in London doesn't know what to do with it. So the job goes to a sign printer. Signs. Part of the attraction was that it wouldn't need glass. Nothing between you and the picture. Now come back to that black. The ink's on the other side. They printed the black first. Then put the white behind it. That smooth surface you've been looking at? You've been looking through it.
