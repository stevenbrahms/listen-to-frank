Eurasia Siberian Symphony 1963

Eurasia Siberian Symphony 1963 by Joseph Beuys, 1966, is in Gallery 408 on Floor 4 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 22 sec.

Joseph Beuys, 1966 · Gallery 408 · Floor 4

That hare’s on stilts. Legs tied to skinny poles, right down to the floor. Joseph Beuys, a German sculptor, used all this in a performance. In Berlin, back in the sixties. On Halloween. So, for Beuys, the performance was the art. This board is just what got left behind. At this point the Berlin Wall’s got the city split in two. And he’s written Eurasia across the board. Europe and Asia, together. Well, most of it. The felt’s covering the end. Above it, half a cross. The bottom’s missing. And the hare? To Beuys, that’s the animal that can make the leap. Get from one side to the other. Now, those triangles. The gray one’s felt. The yellow one’s fat. He says they’re about spiritual warmth. Thinks of himself as a kind of shaman. And his explanation starts with a plane crash. Okay, so during the war, he’s in the back of a German dive bomber over Crimea. It crashes. The pilot dies. Beuys says Tatars find him in the snow. He says he’s unconscious for twelve days. They cover him in fat, wrap him in felt. Warm him up. Keep the heat in. Except, the military records put him in a German field hospital the day after the crash. So, look at the fat and felt again.
