Endless Column, Version I

Constantin Brancusi, 1918 · Floor 6

One shape. Fat in the middle, pinched at both ends. Like a big wooden bead. Stack it. Then stack it again. That’s Brancusi’s Endless Column. And the whole thing is carved oak. You can still see the chisel marks. Now look at the top. The last shape gets cut off halfway through. Same at the bottom. It doesn’t end. It just... stops. And your eye keeps adding pieces, right up through the ceiling. Ya know, usually, a sculpture stands on a pedestal. Brancusi had been using shapes like these underneath his sculptures. Then he starts making the base the whole thing. Nothing on top. Just more base. He makes more columns over the years. Taller ones. The same shape, over and over. Then, in the thirties, a group of women back home in Romania asks the local boy who made it big in Paris to make a war memorial. The Women’s League of Gorj. His home county. So, during the First World War, German troops had tried to cross the bridge at Targu Jiu. Local people helped defend it. Old men, women, kids. Held them off until reinforcements arrived. Now they want something to remember the people who died defending the town. And Brancusi gives them this shape. Cast in iron. Almost a hundred feet tall. Out in the open, with the sky above it. And right at the top, the same half-shape. Already widening out again. Anyway, come back to this one. Six foot eight. It’s the earliest one anybody still has.
