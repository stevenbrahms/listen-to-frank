The False Mirror

René Magritte, 1929 · Gallery 517 · Floor 5

One enormous eye. No lashes. No face to help you out. And where the iris should be, blue sky. Clouds. A perfectly nice day. Look at the inner corner, way over on the left. Brown, wet, shiny. Magritte’s gone to some trouble to make that bit look real. The eyeball’s rounded. Shaded. But the sky doesn’t seem to follow the curve. It just sits there, flat, like a picture inside the eye. Then right in the middle, that black pupil. Not a speck of light on it. So are you looking through this eye, or looking at what’s going on inside it? It’s called The False Mirror. Helpful. The photographer Man Ray owned this. Had it on his wall in Paris. He said it saw as much as it was seen. Then, years later, CBS needs a logo. The network president, this guy Stanton, asks his designer, Bill Golden, to come up with something. Golden comes back with an eye. You know the one. Golden said his source was an old folk-art eye, the kind painted on barns to keep evil away. The CBS eye goes everywhere. Cameras, curtains, buildings. Even jewelry. Then this museum gives Magritte a big show. He comes over from Belgium with his wife and their dog. There’s a black-tie preview. And in the catalogue, the museum makes a claim. This painting, changed around a bit, is the eye millions of people know from CBS. Golden credited the barns. The museum credited Magritte. But look back at the sky for a second. The first still version of the CBS logo had clouds behind it. Stanton had them taken out.
