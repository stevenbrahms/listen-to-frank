Kichka's Breakfast I

Daniel Spoerri, 1960

So… somebody just finished breakfast. Her name’s Kichka. It’s a morning in a little Paris hotel room, and her boyfriend, Daniel Spoerri, has visitors coming. The dishes are still out. Most people would wash up before company. He gets the glue. So look at what he caught. Two white bowls. The coffeepot. A tin of Nescafé. And under it all, not a table. A kitchen chair with a board across the seat. Now the eggcups, red and green. Look close. There’s still shell in them. And at the far left, a little tin lid full of cigarette butts. Coffee. Egg. Cigarette. Maybe that’s how the morning went. He said, “I pasted together the morning’s breakfast, which was still there by chance.” Then he hangs it on the wall. Chair and all. He calls these snare pictures. Traps. One of the very first. Whatever’s lying there gets fixed right where it landed, and he stands the whole thing up. So the board that was lying flat is now facing you. If a painting of breakfast is a picture of a morning, this is the morning. A few years on, he turns a Paris gallery into a restaurant. He cooks. Art critics wait the tables. At the end of the night, a table goes up on the wall, plates and all. And the food side? He ends up calling that Eat Art. Before any of this, Spoerri had a different job. The kind where nothing you do is still there in the morning. Lead dancer in the Bern City Ballet.
