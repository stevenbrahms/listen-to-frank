Untitled (Parabolic Lens)

Fred Eversley, 1971

It's eleven thirty at night. Fred Eversley's in the company parking lot. His MG won't start. So he opens the door, sticks a foot out, and starts pushing along the pavement. Forgets about the telephone pole. The door slams shut on his leg. The car rolls down a gully. Thirteen months on crutches. So, Eversley's an engineer. Builds test labs for Gemini and Apollo. His job was to put a piece of spacecraft inside a test chamber. Make it unbelievably loud. And see what comes loose. While he's recovering, a sculptor neighbor offers him a loft. Free rent. Just help out with the sculptures. Anyway, the art takes over. Three years later, he's got a solo show at the Whitney. And this shape comes with him from engineering. The parabola. A curve that gathers things in and focuses them on one spot. He pours colored liquid plastic into a mold. Then spins it. The liquid climbs up the sides. A dish forms in the middle. Physics takes care of that. Getting it this smooth? That's Eversley. Ninety-nine percent of the work is polishing, he said. Now look at what all that polishing gets you. Three feet of glowing space candy. Start at the purple rim. Work your way through the amber. Down into that blue. Whatever's behind it gets pulled into the curve. Even the shadow has color. It's not really a picture of anything. It's more like engineering, aimed at your eyeballs. He wanted to suck people in. He keeps making these for fifty years. This is one of the first. Though he'd tried the spinning part long before the spacecraft. Before the car. Before any of this. As a kid. With a pie pan on a record player.
