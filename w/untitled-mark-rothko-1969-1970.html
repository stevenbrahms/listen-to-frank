Untitled

Untitled by Mark Rothko, 1969–1970, is in Gallery 403 on Floor 4 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 55 sec.

Mark Rothko, 1969–1970 · Gallery 403 · Floor 4

Nothing taller than three feet. Doctor's orders. Rothko's just had an aneurysm that nearly killed him. That's a lot to ask of Mark Rothko, you know? The color guy. Those big, soft rectangles, taller than the people looking at them. At first, he does what he's told. Smaller work. On paper. That winter, he and his wife split up. He moves into the studio, a carriage house on East Sixty-Ninth Street. The studio's got a skylight, with a parachute rigged under it. He pulls it back and forth to control the daylight. The doctor said nothing about parachutes. Come spring, he's back on canvas. Making paintings like this. Six and a half feet tall. So much for three feet. And the color guy's down to black and gray. Black on top. Gray underneath. A thin white border around the whole thing. That border's new. This is the last series he'll put on canvas. Now start at the border and work your way in. The black barely moves. The gray underneath won't sit still. Big loops, down on the right. That's not a wrist. That's a whole arm. Find the line between them. Looks like he used a ruler. He didn't. The gray keeps scrubbing up into the black. Rough little strokes, all along that edge. And that white border. It started on the paper. The small stuff. He'd tape down the edges, paint, pull the tape. There's your border. He went back to big. The border came with him. People see the moon in these. It's the year of the moon landing, so, sure. Some see a man near the end. Others see a painter starting over. Less than a year later, some murals he's given to the Tate arrive in London. They get there in the morning. That same morning, back in New York, his assistant finds him in the studio. Rothko had taken his own life. He was sixty-six.
