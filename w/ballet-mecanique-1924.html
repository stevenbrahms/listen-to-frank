Ballet Mécanique

Fernand Léger, 1924 · Gallery 505 · Floor 5

Sooner or later, a woman with a sack on her shoulder shows up. Watch her climb those stairs. And then… she's back at the bottom. Same woman. Same sack. Up she goes. Fernand Léger, one of the big painters in Paris, made this with an American filmmaker, Dudley Murphy. The only film Léger took on himself. It starts with Charlie Chaplin. Well, a cut-out of him. Lifts his hat. Disappears. The original title was Chaplin Presents the Mechanical Ballet. Then a woman on a swing. That's Murphy's wife. Then it's off. A whisk, all tangled wires. The wide mouth of a funnel. Light catching a copper pot. And at one point, in a shiny ball, you can catch the two men filming. They've ended up in their own movie. No plot. Léger figured painting had already stopped needing a story. Why couldn't film? Get a camera close enough to something ordinary, and it starts to have a personality. Then cut to something else. See what happens between them. This becomes one of the most influential experimental films ever made. And it was supposed to have music. George Antheil wrote it. Sixteen player pianos. Airplane propellers. A siren. Keeping sixteen player pianos together turns out to be a problem. So the film goes one way. The music goes another. In Paris, the music gets its own concert. There are accounts of fistfights out in the street. Over the music. Léger actually wrote down what he wanted to do to you. First, amaze you. Then make you uneasy. Then keep going until you can't take it anymore.
