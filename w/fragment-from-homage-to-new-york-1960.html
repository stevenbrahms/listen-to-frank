Fragment from Homage to New York

Jean Tinguely, 1960 · Gallery 405 · Floor 4

The sculpture garden, downstairs, on a Thursday evening in March, 1960. A couple hundred invited guests, in their good clothes, standing around a machine twenty-seven feet high. Painted white. All of it. The Swiss artist Jean Tinguely has built it to destroy itself. The year before, he’d put out a manifesto in Düsseldorf. Story goes, a hundred and fifty thousand copies dropped from a plane. The gist? Nothing holds still. So stop pretending anything lasts. Now he’s come to New York to build something with half an hour to live. This guy Billy Klüver, an engineer at Bell Labs, helps him. They round up junk from all over. An old piano. A bathtub. A bassinet. Bicycle wheels. A wooden cable spool. Then they turn it on. Wheels spinning. Metal banging against metal. The piano manages three notes. One painting machine feeds its paper the wrong way. The balloon’s supposed to burst. Doesn’t. The bottles full of stinking liquids are supposed to fall. They stay put. But the piano does catch fire. That part’s planned. Then a little burning cart shoots out from underneath it. Ends up against a reporter’s ladder. He turns it around, and off it goes into NBC’s sound equipment. Meanwhile, Tinguely’s getting worried. There’s a fire extinguisher attached to the burning piano. He thinks it might explode. The fire extinguisher. So he asks for the fireman to put the fire out. Some of the audience think the fireman’s ruining the show. They’ve got it backward. Afterward, people pick through the wreckage. Take parts home. You know, souvenirs. So look at this one. It’s called Fragment from Homage to New York. The wheels. That big wooden cable spool down low. The oil can. A ragged corner of an American flag up top. This was the other cart. Its job was suicide. Roll off into the garden’s pool. Except the motor was too weak. Tinguely had to help it along. Still didn’t make it. And Tinguely knew all along it never would. Never swapped it for a bigger one. It ended up in the collection.
