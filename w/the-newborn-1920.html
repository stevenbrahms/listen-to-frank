The Newborn, Version I

Constantin Brancusi, 1920 · Floor 6

Alright, so get down to its level. It’s about eight inches long. Like something from a spaceship. Or a... droid egg thing. It’s from 1920, and it looks like it hasn’t even been invented yet. And be honest. You’d like to put it in your pocket. Now look at the front. Like somebody’s sliced the end off an egg. Except there’s a little curl left at the bottom. That’s a chin. And that big flat bit? A mouth. Wide open. It’s a baby. If you’ve ever held one while it’s screaming, you know the shape. The whole head goes mouth. Constantin Brancusi made it. Romanian, living in Paris. One of the guys who basically invented modern sculpture. He’s been making children’s heads for years. The early ones look like actual children. You’d know what you were looking at from across the room. Then the heads get simpler. The neck goes. The hair. The ears. More and more of the face. By 1915, he’s carved one in marble that’s down to this. An egg with a cry coming out of it. He calls it The Newborn. Five years later, this bronze. Polished like a mirror. You can still practically hear it. Anyway, he gives this one away. A wedding present to a young couple in Paris. They take it home to New York. A screaming baby. For the newlyweds. MoMA buys it from the bride. Now come back to that shiny surface. Get a little closer. There’s your face. Right in the scream.
