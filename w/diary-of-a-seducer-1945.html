Diary of a Seducer

Diary of a Seducer by Arshile Gorky, 1945, is in Gallery 522 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 49 sec.

Arshile Gorky, 1945 · Gallery 522 · Floor 5

So, people used to say Gorky copied Picasso. Then one summer, he’s out on his in-laws’ farm in Virginia, and he decides to forget the galleries for a while. Look into the grass, he said. So he takes his pencils out into the fields. Draws the stuff growing around his feet. Gives the cows art lectures. Said they were very attentive. Anyway, look at this. Gray. But kinda warm, sorta smoky gray. It’s so thin up top you can see the cloth. And those patches of yellow, buried in it sorta like yolks. Right of center, inside the black. That little white almond with the red slit down the middle. An eye, maybe. Could be an ear. Looks like part of something alive. Hard to say what. And at the far left, one long drip runs straight down toward his signature. He leaves it. His new friends are the Surrealists, washed up in New York by the war. One of them, Max Ernst, reportedly suggests the title. Diary of a Seducer. It comes from Kierkegaard. Story about a man carefully plotting the seduction of a young woman. Well, now you’ve got that in your head. Look at those shapes again. Gorky’s wife said titles didn’t interest him much unless they stirred up something he remembered. So... he keeps this one. In the summer of ’45, he writes his sister. Says for the first time, he can paint without worrying about money. And the guy people said copied Picasso? He’s painting like himself now. Anyway, go back to that shape inside the black. The white one with the red down the middle. Nearly something you recognize. But it won’t quite settle. Back on the farm, he’d bring a drawing home and show his wife. And ask her, does it look like anything you’ve seen before?
