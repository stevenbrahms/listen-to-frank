Barcelona Chair

Ludwig Mies van der Rohe, 1929

So the king of Spain is coming. And you can't just put him in a kitchen chair. That's more or less how the architect explained it. Mies van der Rohe. This is the guy who in 1921 drew a skyscraper with glass for walls. Nobody had built one yet. Anyway, Barcelona, 1929. A world's fair. Mies and the designer Lilly Reich have made Germany's pavilion. Marble walls. A roof that seems to float. A shallow pool. Almost nothing inside. The building's basically the exhibit. And the king and queen are coming for the official reception. Mies says the chair has to be elegant. Monumental. So look at what they make. Polished steel. There was white leather on the original pair. No carving. No gold. Nothing perched on top wearing a crown. But look at the frame from the side. That X. Which turns out is very old. Roman magistrates sat on X-frame seats. Before them, the Egyptians had folding stools built around the same shape. Mies and Reich take that old shape and give it these long steel curves. Looks like the future. But... it's got three thousand years behind it. Okay, so opening day. The king and queen visit. And the story goes, neither one ever sits in the chairs. After the fair, they dismantle the pavilion. The building disappears. But... the chair gets orders. They keep making it. Keep putting it in rooms that want to look important. Corporate lobbies. Law firms. You've probably sat in one, holding a coffee, waiting for somebody to call you in.
