Guitar

Guitar by Pablo Picasso, 1914, is in Gallery 503 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 22 sec.

Pablo Picasso, 1914 · Gallery 503 · Floor 5

Okay, find the hole in the middle of this guitar. On a real guitar, that’s nothing, ’cause, ya know… it’s a hole. Here, it’s a little tube. Sticking straight out at you. Hang on to that. Now look at what the whole thing’s made of. A few pieces of sheet metal, cut and bent. Some wires for strings. Back then, sculpture meant carving it out of stone, or modeling it in clay and casting it. Either way, you start with a solid lump of something and find the thing inside. Picasso’s a painter. The one who’d already broken painting into pieces. He looks at all that and goes, nah. He doesn’t carve it. Doesn’t mold it. He builds it. Like a kid with a cereal box. First out of cardboard, paper and string, and he hangs it on his studio wall, right in with his drawings. Then he does it all again in sheet metal. This one. A poet says people who came by the studio asked: Does it go on a pedestal, or on the wall? Is it a painting, or a sculpture? And Picasso, the story goes, just says: “It’s nothing. It’s a guitar.” Then he keeps it for almost sixty years before he gives it to MoMA. Now look at the wall behind it. The shadows. Painters fake those for a living. This one makes its own. So go back to that hole. Picasso said that tube came from an African mask. A Grebo mask, with eyes that poke out at you.
