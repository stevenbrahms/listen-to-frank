Woman with a Mandolin

Georges Braque, 1937 · Gallery 523 · Floor 5

So Braque had an accordion. Used to play Bach on it. There's sheet music on the stand. More on the table. And a mandolin. In her lap. Black against her black dress. You can almost miss the thing the painting's named after. Now find her face. The profile. The eye. The mouth. Thin, pale lines bringing somebody out of all that black. A few years earlier, Braque had been painting slabs of plaster black, then scratching through them. Every cut brought the white back up. He'd been looking at ancient Greek pottery. Here, he gets that pale-on-dark effect in oil. Then he surrounds her with all this stuff. Green walls. Pink curves. Patterns running into other patterns. Even the picture on the wall has another little roomful of objects inside it. Basically a Matisse with the lights turned off. Braque kept musical instruments around his studio. Spent years painting them. Guitars. Violins. Mandolins. Back when he and Picasso were inventing a whole way of taking them apart on canvas. He liked their shapes. But also what your hands could do with them. An instrument, he said, "comes alive to the touch." Look at hers. Already resting on the mandolin. He paints this in 1937. Three years later, Germany invades France. Braque and his wife load paintings into their car and head south. Find somewhere to hide them. By autumn, they're back in Paris. German officers have moved into the house across the street. And German soldiers have been inside his studio. Nothing's been disturbed. The only thing they've taken is his accordion. Which he hadn't played in years.
