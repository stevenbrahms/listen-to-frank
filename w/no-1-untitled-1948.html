NO. 1 (UNTITLED)

MARK ROTHKO, 1948

This is Rothko right before he becomes Rothko. A few years earlier, his paintings were crowded with strange little creatures. Bodies. Plants. Mythological weirdos. All kinds of things floating around in there. By 1948, who’s left? Nobody. Rothko had decided that almost everything was getting in the way. The people. The objects. The stories. Anything your brain could grab onto and call finished. He said he wanted clarity. Nothing between him and the idea. Nothing between the idea and you. Basically, nothing in the way. Give us a face and we know what to do. Read the expression. Make up the story. Move on. Rothko wanted to take away that exit. So he starts firing things from the painting. The people go. The objects go. The weird little creatures melt into these floating patches that people later called Multiforms. And look at the surface. Thin washes of paint soaking into the canvas, one over another. Green under blue. Orange pushing through tan. Until the light starts to feel like it’s coming from somewhere inside the painting. None of this is casual. You can feel him looking, changing something, stepping back, looking again. This isn’t a guy who can’t paint a face. This is a guy getting rid of everything he knows how to paint, on purpose, to see what’s left. And within a year or two, these patches stop drifting. They square up. They start stacking themselves. The rectangles are moving in.
