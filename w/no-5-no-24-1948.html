NO. 5/NO. 24

MARK ROTHKO, 1948

Two numbers. No name. No. 5. Or No. 24. It’s both. That barely counts as a title. It sounds like something written on the side of a crate. Right around now, Rothko starts dropping the titles. The logic was pretty simple. Give a painting a name and the name gets there before you do. Call it Sunset, everybody finds the sunset, and the looking is basically over. Call it No. 5, and you’re on your own. Just you and the thing. He’d already stopped painting people. Now even the names start disappearing. First the figures go. Then the titles. So look at what’s here, with nothing to call it. A brown band across the middle. Black shapes, like something got burned. A pale field holding it all up. Orange pushing in around the edges. Now, one fact about 1948, said plain and then left alone. That October, Rothko’s mother died. He never said this painting was about that. So we don’t get to. But this is the kind of thing he was making that year. No name. No figure. No story waiting to explain itself. Nothing tells you what happened. Nothing tells you how to feel about it. It just sits there.
