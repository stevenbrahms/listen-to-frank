Garden in Sochi

Arshile Gorky, 1941 · Gallery 522 · Floor 5

You're gonna start naming things. Bird. Face. Boot. Maybe a fish. Don't get too attached. This is Garden in Sochi. Gorky made three paintings with that title. But there's a small problem. He'd never been to Sochi. He wasn't Russian either. He was born Vosdanig Adoian, in an Armenian village called Khorkom. In America, he starts calling himself Arshile Gorky. Even tells people he's related to Maxim Gorky, the Russian writer. He isn't. And Maxim Gorky was a pen name too. But the garden came from somewhere real. When Gorky was a kid, the Armenian Genocide drove his family out. A few years later, his mother died of starvation. He and his sister made it to America. He never saw Khorkom again. His father had a garden there. Old apple trees that stopped giving fruit. Wild carrots growing in the shade. Porcupines nesting there too. And a blue rock, half buried in the earth. Village women would rub their breasts against it to make a wish. Nearby, a holy tree hung with strips of cloth people had torn from their clothes. He called it the Garden of Wish Fulfillment. So that's the place behind this painting. He just gave it a Russian address. The painters around him in New York are reaching for myths. Gorky reaches for his dad's garden. So, look again. That dark blue shape near the middle. Maybe you're seeing the rock. Those other shapes start looking like things growing. Or parts of a body. Knowing the story won't turn it into a picture you can label. But suddenly there's a place in there. Apple trees. Shade. People coming to make a wish. And when MoMA asks him to explain what's in the painting, this guy with the Russian name writes: "My father had a little garden."
