ARK

JOEL SHAPIRO, 2020 / 2023–24

Eighteen feet across. Almost twelve feet tall. Not one piece of it standing up straight. For about fifty years, Joel Shapiro made people. Little blocky figures. Arms and legs out of rectangular beams, caught mid-stride or mid-fall. He made a person out of four blocks and made you believe it. That little switch your brain makes. One second, a person. The next, just a bunch of wood stuck together. Now count the people in this one. There aren't any. But the switch keeps happening. You keep waiting for these slabs to turn into a body. They never quite do. Red, mostly. One blue. A grey one cutting down through the middle. And a yellow blade going up, higher than everything else. One part shoves. Another braces. Another throws its weight out over the floor. The empty space is carrying as much as the wood is. Nothing in here is resting. And the color isn't paint, exactly. It's casein. Milk protein. It dries dead matte. He finished this one in 2024. He died the following June, at eighty-three. He called it ARK. The boat you build when you can see what's coming. Except this one doesn't look built to float. It looks caught in the second before everything hits the floor. But nothing does. All that weight. All those directions. Still holding.
