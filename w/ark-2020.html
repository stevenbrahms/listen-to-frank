Ark

Joel Shapiro, 2020 / 2023–24 · Gallery 400 · Floor 4

So this thing is eighteen feet across. Almost twelve feet tall. And nothing is standing up straight. So for decades, Joel Shapiro was known for these blocky little people. Caught mid-step or just about to fall over. A handful of blocks, and you’ve got a person. Then you look again, and it’s just blocks. He could keep you going back and forth like that. Then, after September Eleventh, he starts breaking apart the little people and models in his studio. Pulls them to pieces. Puts the pieces together again. And over the years, the body gets harder to find. Now count the people in this one. There aren’t any. But you’re still looking. That could be an arm. That could be a leg. Something’s almost coming together. Then you move a little, and it’s gone. This is ARK. Finished in 2024. Red, mostly. A couple of blues. A gray one cutting down through the middle. And that yellow blade going up above everything else. Look where each piece puts its weight. One shoves into another. One holds another one up. Another sticks way out over the floor. Now walk around it. Keep looking for something that’ll pull all those pieces into a body. You start watching the gaps as much as the wood. And look at the surface. He paints it with casein. Basically, paint made from milk. Dries with zero shine. Nothing to distract you. Just those big flat colors, going every which way. ARK. Doesn’t look built to float, does it? Looks caught in the second before everything hits the floor. But nothing does. And he had his doubts about it. Every piece got made on its own. He figured each one could be a sculpture by itself. Worried the whole thing wouldn’t be as interesting as the pieces. Then he started putting them together. Turned out, he was wrong.
