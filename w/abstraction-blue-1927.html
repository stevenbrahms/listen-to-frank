Abstraction Blue

Abstraction Blue by Georgia O'Keeffe, 1927, is in Gallery 509 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 55 sec.

Georgia O'Keeffe, 1927 · Gallery 509 · Floor 5

So, she mails the drawings to a friend in New York. Then... she gets scared. She’s twenty-seven, teaching art at a little college in South Carolina. She’s put aside everything she was taught and started making shapes in charcoal. She writes to her friend that she’s scared nobody will get them. And scared somebody actually will. Anyway, find that white line. The friend takes the drawings to Alfred Stieglitz, a photographer who runs a gallery. Then Stieglitz just hangs them up without asking. O’Keeffe eventually finds out and tells him to take them down. But he won’t. That show gets her career going. Everything you know her for comes later. Flowers, cow skulls, the desert. Dorm posters. Stieglitz falls for her. Photographs her. Shows the photographs, including a bunch of nudes. And with Freud the hot new thing, suddenly every art critic’s a doctor. O’Keeffe complains they’ve turned her into some unearthly creature floating around in the air. Then she paints the big flowers, and the critics see sex. She paints more flowers. More sex. She could’ve painted a filing cabinet. They’d have found something. But through all this, she keeps making abstract pictures too. And right in the middle of the flowers, she makes this. Come back to the white line. It’s wide at the top, then thins almost to a hair. On the left, a minty green shape, cupped in dark blue. On the right, almost its other half, only grayer. Like somebody cut it down the middle and put it back a little off. She once said shapes and colors could say things she had no words for. Look where the two curves meet the white line. They almost line up. Almost.
