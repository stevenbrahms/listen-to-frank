Bricklayer

Bricklayer by August Sander, 1928, is in Gallery 514 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 40 sec.

August Sander, 1928 · Gallery 514 · Floor 5

He's looking right at you. A whole stack of bricks across his shoulders on a board. His head sitting right in the middle. Kinda like they bricked him in. And he keeps the load up there for the picture. He's got one hand on his hip. Such a strong look. No name in the title. Just the job. And in German, it's Handlanger. The helper. The guy that just carries the bricks over. So August Sander runs a portrait studio in Cologne. And he's decided he's going to photograph all of Germany. Farmers. Bankers. A pastry cook. Artists. Cops. Whether you're famous or a nobody, everybody gets a place in the system. A dozen or so portraits in each section. Sander puts sixty of them in a book. Calls it the Face of Our Time. Then the Nazis seize it and destroy the printing plates. They've got a whole theory about what a German face looks like. And here's this book full of all these different German faces. By then, Sander's oldest son, Erich, is already in prison. He'd been handing out leaflets against the Nazis. They give him ten years for treason. And inside, they put him to work as the prison photographer. He takes pictures of the other prisoners. And himself. All this time, he's writing home. Years into the sentence, he finds ways to get letters past the censors. Some written in invisible ink. Then, with his sentence almost up, his appendix bursts. He doesn't get treatment in time. He dies before he gets out. And the last photograph in this museum's set? Erich's death mask.
