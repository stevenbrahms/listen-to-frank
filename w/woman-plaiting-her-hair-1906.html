Woman Plaiting Her Hair

Pablo Picasso, 1906 · Gallery 502 · Floor 5

She’s doing her hair. That’s it. But look at the face. Those flat cheeks, the hard edge of the nose. Eyes half shut. Nothing up there is moving. Then this big arm comes across her chest to gather up the hair. The title says she’s braiding it. So, give her a minute. She’s still getting it all together. Picasso was trained to paint. Sculpture, he had to figure out. Borrow somebody’s tools, work in somebody else’s studio. And around the time he paints this, one of those places is a kitchen. His friend Paco Durrio’s. A Basque potter up in Montmartre, with a kiln in his kitchen. Durrio’s been after him to try ceramics. So Picasso makes a woman doing her hair. And they fire her in the kitchen. They call her his first confirmed ceramic. A woman doing the same ordinary thing as this one, except you can pick her up. Now look back at the painting. Start with that solid head and come down. Across the arm. Down through the belly. Kinda warm, round. Still got real weight. Then there’s that thin orange line across her hips. Maybe a ledge. Keep going. Where’d she go? White sheet. Pink and grey paint. A few quick black scribbles where you were expecting legs. He gives you all that weight up top, and down here there’s barely anything holding her up. It’s like he started looking at a body the way a sculptor would. He’d already made a woman out of clay by the time he’d painted that arm. And she still hasn’t finished her hair.
