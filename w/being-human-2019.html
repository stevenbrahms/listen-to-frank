Being Human

Christopher Kulendran Thomas, 2019 · Gallery 213 · Floor 2

Sooner or later, Taylor Swift shows up on that glass. But it's not her. Close, though. She's sitting in a cane chair. Hotel bed behind her. A stone Buddha head on a little table. A computer put her together from interviews it found online. And she's projected onto glass. You can see the room right through her. Keep an eye on that room. Christopher Kulendran Thomas made this with Annika Kuhlmann. It's called Being Human. Then the film puts you on a train. A young Tamil artist is talking about Kant. Human rights. He's real. Sri Lanka's civil war lasted twenty-six years. It ended with a massacre of Tamil civilians. Kulendran Thomas grew up in London. His family is Tamil. They'd left Sri Lanka as the violence got worse. And around the time the war ended, something else was getting started in Colombo. An art market. A biennale. New galleries. People buying. Now, Kulendran Thomas has a problem with how this works. He says artists like him get rewarded for performing where they're from. Make something that looks like what the buyer thinks Sri Lanka looks like. The postcard version. Then he goes shopping. Five paintings and sculptures by Sri Lankan artists. All from one gallery in Colombo. Two are by Kingsley Gunatillake. Then he adds another. Looks like a Gunatillake. Gunatillake didn't make it. Kulendran Thomas did, using a computer trained on work by Sri Lankan artists. And he puts it in the room with the others. The room you've been looking at through Taylor Swift. Six works. One didn't come from the gallery. Go on. See if you can tell. And the thing they've given the fake Taylor Swift to talk about? Being real.
