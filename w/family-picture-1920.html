Family Picture

Family Picture by Max Beckmann, 1920, is in Gallery 514 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 45 sec.

Max Beckmann, 1920 · Gallery 514 · Floor 5

So there's six people in one room. See if you can find two looking at each other. That's Beckmann on the piano bench. White cloth round his head. He's holding a horn. Not playing it. Just holding it, like he's forgotten what it's for. Sorta looks like they're getting ready for Carnival. His wife Minna's at the mirror, in her corset and striped stockings. Her face is the gray one in the glass. Her mother's got a hand over her face. Her sister, at the table, looks straight out at you. Their son Peter's on the floor with a book. Seems like he found something else to do. And the woman with the newspaper is a relative who helped around the house. Some candles burning on the table. The one on the piano's gone out. But it's still smoking. A family portrait is usually supposed to say one word. Fine. Look how fine they are. Anyway, when the Nazis cleared modern art out of German museums, they took down more than five hundred of Beckmann's works. He paints this a couple of years after the First World War. During the war, he'd volunteered as a medical orderly. Months of patching up other men. Then he came apart himself. Had a breakdown. Went to Frankfurt. In a letter during the war, he writes about filling up the foreground with junk. So you don't have to look into the frightening space beyond it. Now look at the floor. A kite. A candle. A box. Minna's mother, her sister, the relative with the paper. They lived together in Berlin. Beckmann didn't. He was in Frankfurt. And Minna was in Austria, singing Wagner at the opera in Graz. He put them both in anyway. A few years later, they divorce. He marries somebody else. But he kept on painting... her.
