Pines and Rocks (Fontainebleau?)

Paul Cézanne, 1897 · Gallery 501 · Floor 5

The title of this one is Pines and Rocks. Then, in parentheses, Fontainebleau… question mark. So… what’s up with the question mark? Fontainebleau’s an actual forest near Paris. But for years, people thought this was Provence. Cézanne’s home turf. Other end of the country. Look at those rusty red pines. Grey rocks going pink. Blue poking through the branches. The paint gives you a little more to go on. Look at the pale gaps between the brushstrokes. That’s just bare canvas. And where there is paint, it’s thin. Almost like watercolor. They used to think he painted this near the end of his life. But that thin paint didn’t fit the thick, slanting strokes they were comparing it with. So now they put it around seven years earlier, when he’s spending time near Paris. So… the question mark stays. But those bare patches are interesting for another reason. A couple of years before he painted this, Cézanne finally gets his own show. He’s fifty-six. His old friend Pissarro goes to see the show and writes his son afterward. Says some of the still lifes are perfect. But there are others that look like they aren’t even finished yet, and he likes those even better. Later, a young Matisse spends real money on one. Near the end of his life, Cézanne writes a friend about the color, the light. Those little places where one thing meets another. He can’t quite get it all down. Can’t cover the whole canvas. The paintings stay incomplete. Now look back at those gaps. He was already leaving them here. Years before he tried to explain them.
