Tropical Night

Christopher Cozier, 2006–2014

Looks like somebody took a notebook apart and clipped the pages to the wall. Each drawing a little bigger than a paperback. Two hundred and sixty-eight altogether. Keep an eye out for the grapes. Little bunches of them. They'll come back. Christopher Cozier is from Trinidad. He's traveling a lot, so his studio becomes wherever he can sit down. A hotel room. A friend's place. These pages are small enough to take along. And he's tried building up a series like this before. Twice. Had to sell the drawings off to support himself. Then a grant gives him enough money to hold on to the next set. So he keeps at it for years. Calls it Tropical Night. Sounds nice. Warm breeze. A drink with some fruit in it. Basically the Caribbean made for people with return tickets. That's the version he's pushing back on. The colorful, fruit-filled one. Then you get close. Little figures. Fences. Writing crowded around the images. A map keeps turning up. Bits of the world following him from room to room. And he keeps changing what goes next to what. That's why the clips matter. Every time the work goes up, he can rearrange it according to what's happening in the world. Or how he's feeling. Same drawings. New connections. Now, those grapes. Cozier borrowed them from the cover of Nelson's West Indian Readers. A schoolbook from a British publisher. First published in 1928. Generations of Caribbean children learned to read from it. Anyone who grew up with that book might know those grapes on sight. Now he's put that familiar little picture among his own drawings. His notes. His travels. What's happening around him. And every time he shows these pages, he can rearrange them. Change what goes next to what. That old schoolbook told you which page came next. Here, he gets to decide.
