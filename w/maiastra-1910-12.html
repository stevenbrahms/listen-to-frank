Maiastra

Constantin Brancusi, 1910-12 · Floor 6

Alright, so the story goes that Constantin Brancusi walked to Paris from… Bucharest. Fifteen hundred miles. Sleeping outside. Nobody quite agrees how long the whole thing took. Let's call it a year. Supposedly, he arrives on Bastille Day. Later, he writes that France welcomed him with a brass band and a military salute. He's a peasant's kid from the Carpathian Mountains. Three years later, he's working for Rodin. The Thinker guy. Brancusi lasts about a month. "Nothing grows under the shadow of big trees," he says. So he leaves. And a few years later, there's this bird. The Măiastră. A magical bird from Romanian fairy tales. Golden feathers. A song with powers. Exactly what powers… depends on who's telling the story. Here, she's white marble. About two feet tall. Chest out. Neck stretched. Beak aimed at the ceiling. Looks like she's about to let the whole room have it. It's his first bird. He'll make about thirty. But look at what's underneath her. Almost six feet of base. Three times as tall as the bird. Go past that block at the top. Under it. Two people. Rough stone. Barely carved. Holding a rock on their heads. Brancusi made them too. They were a sculpture in their own right. He'd already shown them. Then he puts them to work holding up a bird. It's the first time he builds one sculpture into another. He'll keep doing it for the rest of his life. Now go back up to the bird. Later, he says the hardest part was her head. Getting it up like that without her looking proud. Or like she's picking a fight.
