Tears

Kay WalkingStick, 1990

America's getting ready to throw itself a five-hundred-year party. The Columbus quincentennial. Five hundred years since a European guy showed up. Kay WalkingStick is Cherokee. She said she couldn't possibly celebrate Columbus. So she makes this. Small enough to carry in your arms. Four black poles. A platform. A little bundle lying on top. And some words on the base. It's a model of a funerary scaffold. WalkingStick described how Native people who moved with the seasons would lay their dead on a platform like this and burn them. The ashes return to nature. The smoke rises. Something going down. Something going up. Now look at that bundle. It's meant to look like a person. Inside, there's a corn husk. Some turquoise. A few stones. And the leather around it came from a shirt. One she'd made for her husband, Michael. They'd been married thirty years. He died suddenly, the year before she made this. She still had the shirt. So that's what she wraps around the little figure. Then underneath the platform, where the fire would go, she puts a copper plate. It says: "In 1492, we were twenty million. Now, we are two million." And then a question. "Where are the generations?"
