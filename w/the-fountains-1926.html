The Fountains

The Fountains by Agnes Pelton, 1926, is in Gallery 509 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 47 sec.

Agnes Pelton, 1926 · Gallery 509 · Floor 5

Agnes Pelton paints this inside a windmill. An actual windmill. Out on Long Island. She moves in around forty, after her mother dies. For most of her life, they've lived together. Now she's got this strange old building to herself. She earns money painting portraits of the people who come out for the summer. Faces. Clothes. Somebody sitting in front of her. And then there's this. So, where's the light coming from? That big yellow thing in the back? The glowing ball down below? The white spray climbing through the middle? Everything seems to be lighting everything else. She's in her mid-forties. This is The Fountains. One of her first abstractions. She's been reading spiritual books. Meditating. Paying attention to the images that come to her. And trying to work out how to paint them. But... there's a painting problem. A thought doesn't have a face. You can't sit it by a window and see where the shadows fall. So, she gives it water. Mist. These strange glowing shapes. Things that won't quite hold still. And originally, the title had another word in it. 'Love.' For her, these were real energies. Working on everything alive, even if you couldn't point to them. So come back to that white spray. Looks like water turning into light. Or the other way around. On her sketch for this painting, she gives it a label. 'Think spray.' She keeps exploring this kind of painting for the rest of her life. Later, out in the California desert. She once compares herself to the keeper of a little lighthouse. Sending out a beam that goes farther than she knows.
