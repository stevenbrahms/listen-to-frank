Untitled

Kazuo Shiraga, 1964

So there's a rope hanging from a studio ceiling in Japan. Kazuo Shiraga grabs it with both hands. Underneath him, a canvas on the floor. Paint piled onto it. He lowers his feet into the paint. Then just swings. That's how he paints. Seriously. He'd tried brushes. Then his hands. Then his feet. By the time he makes this one, he's been foot painting for about ten years. Now look at that big sweeping arc on the right. You can almost feel the swing that made it. And the paint's so thick it casts shadows. His weight pushes it out. It piles up. His feet slide through it. The paint pushes back. He's part of Gutai, a group of postwar Japanese artists trying to make something nobody had made before. This was his answer. And look what comes out. Almost entirely red. But never just one red. Bright in one place. Dark as dried blood in another. Then, bottom left, those tiny black characters. His signature. Sitting perfectly still beside all that churn. He paints this in '64. Seven years later, he becomes a Buddhist monk on Mount Hiei. Gets a new name. Sōdō. It means Simple Path. Then comes more training. Thirty-five days of religious exercises. Very little sleep. And when he gets home, he said his wife notices something. She tells him his feet look like they're just treading. No power. And she has a theory. He hasn't had a drink in more than ten months. Maybe he'd better have one. So he does. And after three days, he says, he's back where he started. He's laughing when he tells that part of the story.
