MEASUREMENT ROOM

MEL BOCHNER, 1969

People walk in here and do the little museum panic. Wait. Is this it? Yeah. That's it. Mel Bochner measured the room. That's the work. Black tape along the walls and doorways. Numbers at the edges. The width of that opening, stated on the opening. A room wearing its own receipts. Look closely at the numbers. They're not handwritten. They're Letraset. Rub-down transfer lettering, the stuff ad agencies and architects used before computers. A museum room is built to disappear. White walls. Quiet floor. Nothing to look at but the art. The room gets to pretend it isn't in the conversation. Bochner makes it talk. He did it first in Munich, May 1969, at Galerie Heiner Friedrich. MoMA bought it in 1997. But there isn't one Measurement Room they can pull out of storage. Every time it goes up, the room has to be measured again. Fresh tape. Fresh numbers. This version only fits this space. To the inch. And measurement feels like the most innocent thing in the world. Just the facts. Except now the facts are crawling all over the walls. Half the New Yorkers in here are already doing the math on the square footage. Nothing about the room has changed. You just can't stop seeing it. Bochner died in February 2025. He was eighty-four. Later that year, MoMA measured this room again in his memory. Check the gallery number on your way out. Gallery 421 has an official name now. Mel Bochner's Measurement Room.
