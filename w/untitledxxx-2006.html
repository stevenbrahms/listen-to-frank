Untitled

Untitled by Wade Guyton, 2006, is in MoMA's collection; it isn't listed in a gallery right now. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 48 sec.

Wade Guyton, 2006

So Wade Guyton makes his art on a printer. Has since 2002. This one starts in Microsoft Word. Types an X. Repeats it. Makes it bigger. Then sends the image to a big Epson inkjet printer. Except he's feeding it primed linen. The thick stuff you'd normally stretch over a wooden frame and paint on. But the printer is not happy. It drags. It slips. The ink pools. Guyton has to help it through. Coax it. Pull the fabric. Now look at the right side. Clean. Crisp edges. They showed up on time and followed the instructions. Then the left side. They bunched together. Smear. Land on top of each other. Same basic shape. A very different afternoon. And Guyton keeps those marks. That smear looks like somebody lost their temper with a brush. It came out of a printer. But he's the one who put the wrong material in there. Kept working it through. Decided the result was worth stretching onto a frame. And once it's stretched, that's it. He can't go back into it. His words: he has to live with it, smears and all. So MoMA bought it the year after he made it. Hung it in a show they called What Is Painting? Fair question. Now, one more thing. This canvas is seven feet tall. Almost six feet wide. Too wide for the printer. Look down the middle. He folded the linen in half. Printed one side. Turned it over. Printed the other. Those two halves went through separately. One mostly behaved. The other, you've seen. The printer never saw the whole painting. Neither did Guyton, until he unfolded it.
