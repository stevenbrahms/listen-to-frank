The Starry Night

Vincent van Gogh, 1889 · Gallery 501 · Floor 5

Alright, so find the big white star. Left of the middle, just to the right of that dark tree. The one with all that light around it. That’s the one he wrote his brother Theo about. You know this guy. Vincent van Gogh. When he paints this, he’s a patient in an asylum in the south of France. Checked himself in. Iron bars on his bedroom window. So one morning, way before sunrise, he looks out and sees that light. The morning star, he tells Theo. It looked very big. That’s Venus. The planet. So that part’s real. But the town? He couldn’t see it from there. He put it in, using other views. And that dark tree, the cypress. He pulls it much closer. Follow it up. Almost to the top edge. He’s got a spare room to paint in. A night sky, painted during the day. Now follow that big blue curl across the middle. All those little brush marks turn with it. Even the light around the stars goes round and round. That’s a lot of sky for one little town. That fall, he sends the painting to Theo. Same brother who’s been sending him money to keep him going, because the paintings hardly ever sell. Theo writes back. Calls it the village in the moonlight. He gets what Vincent’s after, he says. But all this searching for style takes away the real feeling of things. Vincent writes back. In the long run, he says, “you’d get used to it.” He’s not sure Theo’s gonna like what he’s doing now. Anyway, go back to that big white star. All that light around it. Five months after he paints it, Vincent writes to his painter friend. Says he’s let himself make the stars too big again. He calls it another failure.
