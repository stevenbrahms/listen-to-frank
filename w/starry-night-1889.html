The Starry Night

Vincent van Gogh, 1889 · Gallery 501 · Floor 5

Alright, so find the big white star. Left of the middle, just to the right of that dark tree. The one with all that light around it. That’s the one he wrote his brother Theo about. You know this guy. Vincent van Gogh. More than two thousand paintings and drawings in about ten years. Sells hardly anything. Sales have… you know… picked up. Anyway, when he paints this, he’s a patient in an asylum in the south of France. Checked himself in. Iron bars on his bedroom window. One morning, way before sunrise, he looks out, and there’s this light. He writes to Theo about that big morning star. That’s actually Venus. The planet. But that town? He couldn’t see it from there. He put it in. He pulls that dark tree way closer than it really was. And up in the sky, he changes the shapes and turns up the glow. Back in Arles, he tells Theo the stars are like the black dots on a map. Why should the lights up there be any harder to get to? You take the train to the next town over. You take death to go to a star… Dying of old age? That’s walking. So… he paints it in a spare room. A night sky, painted during the day. Short strokes, one after another, looping around that big swirl. Around every star, rings of white and yellow, pushed out and out. So go back to that white light. The one that looked very big. That November, he writes to a painter friend. Says he’s made the stars too big again. He calls it… another failure.
