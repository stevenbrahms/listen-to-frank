THE STARRY NIGHT

VINCENT VAN GOGH, 1889

He called it a failure. The most famous night sky there is, and the guy who made it figured he'd blown it. It's June, 1889. He's inside an asylum in the south of France. Not a metaphor. He's a patient. Checked himself in six weeks earlier. And he didn't even do it at night. Broad daylight, indoors, from memory. They gave him a spare room in there to use as a studio. So this isn't a guy out under the stars. It's a guy in a cell, remembering them. The one thing in here he actually saw? That big star. A couple weeks earlier, he wrote his brother about it. The morning star, coming up before dawn. Said it looked huge. That's the one. The rest of that sky came out of his own head. Now look down. That little town under the hills? There was no town out his window. He made it up. And that church? The steeple's not French. It's Dutch. The kind of church he grew up with, back in Holland. He never made it home. So put it together. Up top, a storm that won't shut off. Down below, the most peaceful little town you ever saw, one he couldn't see out any window, dreamed up inside a place with no peace in it. He never explained the gap between the two. Barely said a word about this painting, ever. He didn't have to. And then the rest of us showed up. Put it on mugs. Called it soothing. The most agitated thing in this building became the most calming thing in the world. Not in spite of the agitation. Because of it. Everybody's got a sky they can't turn off. He's just the one who made his beautiful enough to hang over your couch. We fell in love with the part he had to invent. He sent it off with an apology.
