Poetic Shelter

Lygia Clark, 1964

Alright, so this one sits low. These big, dark, rounded shapes, kind of like lily pads. And that top one comes up. Get down a little and have a look underneath. There’s this tiny dark space. Lygia Clark called it Poetic Shelter. The thing about Clark is, she keeps making something, then she gets suspicious of what she’s just made. She’s Brazilian, and she thinks people have gone numb. Forgotten what it’s like to smell something. What it feels like to get hurt. So just looking isn’t enough for her. She’s making sculptures you fold yourself. Paper you keep cutting until you can’t cut anymore. And she tells this story about a basket. Opens it up, and what gets her is the hole. Most people are looking for what’s inside. But to her, the basket’s the wicker and the hole in the middle. You need both. She calls it the empty-full. Now find that slit near the top. The top piece comes right up through it. Look at its edge. She cut it and lifted it. And now the hole’s got a roof. Another time, she’s in a car accident. Breaks her wrist. They tell her to keep it wrapped in a plastic bag. When the bag comes off, she hangs onto it. Blows it up. Puts a stone on it. A bag of nothing, with something sitting on top. You press, and the stone sinks in. Let go, and the air pushes it back. She said it was like a birth. Years later, she’s putting bags of air on people. In therapy sessions.
