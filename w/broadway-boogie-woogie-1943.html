Broadway Boogie Woogie

Piet Mondrian, 1943

So, Mondrian ran on a theory. Straight lines only. Up and down, side to side. Red, yellow and blue, held apart by thick black bars. For about twenty years, that’s him. The grid everybody knows. Now look at this one. No black. Anywhere. So what happened? The war chases him to New York. He’s pushing seventy. First night in town, a friend puts on some piano music, out of Black America. The left hand pounds the same pattern, over and over. The right hand goes wherever it wants. Mondrian falls for it on the spot. He goes dancing. Real fancy steps. And he holds his partner way out at arm’s length. Not the easiest guy to dance with. Look at the yellow lines. Chopped into little blocks of red and blue and gray. Run your eye along one. It doesn’t glide. It hops. A writer friend watched him make this one. One day she finds him putting a yellow box into the middle of a red one. She says, but that doesn’t go with your theory. He says, does it work? Steps back. Looks. Yeah, it works. A little later he tells her the paintings always came first. The theory came after. So, now it seems like we’re gonna have to change the theory. Find the red box with the yellow one inside. Up and right of center, between two blues. Only one like it in the whole painting. It’s the last painting he ever finished. The next winter he died, already tearing into another canvas. That spring, a magazine prints what he’d said about this one. There is still too much of the old in it.
