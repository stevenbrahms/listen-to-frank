Untitled Film Stills

Cindy Sherman, 1977–1980

That’s Cindy Sherman on the kitchen floor. She’s the photographer, too. Groceries spilling out of the bag. One hand on the eggs. Looking off at something we can’t see. Looks like you’ve walked in halfway through a movie. She starts making these when she’s twenty-three, right after she gets to New York. Puts on a wig, turns the apartment into somewhere else. Throws some fabric over the bed, and ya know, now it’s a hotel room. A couple of clip lights on crappy old tripods. A cord so she can fire the camera herself. She wanted the first ones to look like they cost fifty cents. Like those little publicity photos a studio sends out to sell a movie. Except there’s no movie. Seventy pictures. She’s made up every woman in them. And you start filling it in. Maybe she’s just realized she trusted the wrong man. About forty-five seconds too late. She’s barely got the groceries in, and you’ve given her a whole life. Ya know, you’ve seen her a hundred times, but she was never in anything. People have been arguing about what that means ever since. She keeps making these for three years. Sometimes friends help. On a family vacation, she even gets her dad to press the shutter while she directs. Then she just stops. Says she started repeating herself. But... she keeps turning into other people. Fashion models. Women from old paintings. Wealthy ladies posing for their portraits. She doesn’t consider them self-portraits. Says she’s trying to erase herself.
