Jeannette (III)

Jeannette (III) by Henri Matisse, 1911, is in Gallery 506 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 37 sec.

Henri Matisse, 1911 · Gallery 506 · Floor 5

Start with the one that still looks like somebody you might meet. Then walk along the row. That’s Jeanne. She’s recovering from an illness near the Matisses’ house. While she gets better, she sits for him. In the first one, you’ve got soft cheeks. A face. The second one’s still her. The nose has moved over a bit. You’d probably still recognize her on the street. Now this one. Number three. The first two stop at the neck. Here, he gives her shoulders. A chest. And three big bunches of hair. That one on top looks almost like a beret. Keep going. By the fourth, the face is getting longer. And then there’s the fifth. One eye’s practically a glob. The nose is a blade. And whatever’s happening on top of her head, it’s travelled quite a way from a hairstyle. From the right angle, you’re looking at a bronze maraca. Usually, somebody sits for a bust and expects to recognize themselves. Matisse keeps moving things around. He’s already famous as a painter. Sculpture’s where he works things out. And in clay, if you want to move a cheek, you move it. Five heads. Six years. You can check back with the first one, see how much of Jeanne’s still there. But Jeanne herself only sat for the first two. After that, he’s looking at the heads he’s already made. Now, number five. Years later, when he starts it, he doesn’t work from number four. He goes back to a plaster cast of the third. This one. The shoulders. The three bunches of hair. So it looks like a neat little progression. One, two, three, four, five. He skipped right over four.
