The Blue Window

The Blue Window by Henri Matisse, 1913, is in Gallery 506 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 39 sec.

Henri Matisse, 1913 · Gallery 506 · Floor 5

Everything’s blue. The walls. The table. The trees outside. Even the sky, which at least has an excuse. Now, you’re in the bedroom. The garden’s out there. Usually, the window helps keep track of that. Try finding it. There are clues. But the tabletop and the sky are practically the same blue. And see that yellow head sitting on the table? Follow the black line coming out of it. Straight up. Is that the window frame? Or a tree trunk? It’s doing both jobs. Then there’s the green lamp. With what looks like a tree growing out of it. Somebody once asked Matisse on the radio why he painted so many windows. He said he didn’t separate inside from outside. From the horizon right into the room, it was all one space. And this is his room. The bedroom he shares with his wife, Amélie. Her dressing table. Her flowers. Down in the corner, her little brooch in a dish. All soaked in blue. Now look outside. That pale building with a touch of yellow. His studio. The only painting where he shows it from the outside. So you’re standing where he sleeps, looking toward where he works. Same blue all the way through. Matisse said he originally meant this for a Paris fashion designer. The designer says no. Too advanced for him. The next spring, the painting appears in a magazine. With a different title. La Glace sans tain. A mirror without its silver backing. The stuff that makes it reflect. Take that off, and you can see through it. A window.
