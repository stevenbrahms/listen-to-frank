Flag

Jasper Johns, 1954-55 · Gallery 408 · Floor 4

Alright, so before Jasper Johns paints this, he destroys almost everything else he’s made. And… he’s only twenty-four. The way he tells it later, he’s done trying to become an artist. It’s time to actually be one. Then he has this dream. That he’s painting a big American flag. So the next morning, he goes out and buys the materials. A bedsheet. And a can of furniture paint. Except the paint takes too long to dry. He wants to put a stroke down and have it stay there while he does the next one. So he tries hot wax with the color mixed in. It’s called encaustic. Sets as it cools. So he dips strips of newspaper and cloth in the wax, then lays them onto the sheet, inside pencil lines. Up close, you can read it. Try the red stripes, bottom left. At this point the big New York painters are making these enormous abstract things. And Johns picks something every kid can draw. Ya know… one less job. He doesn’t have to design the thing. The stars go here. The stripes go there. There’s only forty-eight. Alaska and Hawaii aren’t in yet. After this, it’s targets. Numbers. Maps. Later, he’d say it all began with a picture of a flag. At his first show, MoMA wants it. But the museum’s board is looking at an American flag and worrying it might be unpatriotic. So a friend of the museum buys it, hangs onto it for years, then gives it to MoMA anyway. And underneath all that worry, the newspaper’s still in there. Stock reports. Ads. Some famous Hollywood type telling you how to slim down. And a recipe for applesauce.
