Two Nudes

Two Nudes by Pablo Picasso, 1906, is in Gallery 502 on Floor 5 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 23 sec.

Pablo Picasso, 1906 · Gallery 502 · Floor 5

Picasso knows exactly how to make a body look graceful. And then he makes this. Two naked women packed into a space that feels about a foot too small. Elbows out. Shoulders up. They're not hugging. They look like two people who've been stuck in the same room way too long. And the whole thing feels heavy. Awkward. Almost clumsy. Like Picasso forgot how to be Picasso. But, he didn't. He'd just come back from Gósol, this little village up in the Pyrenees, and something had changed up there. The bodies got bigger. Simpler. More stone than person. Then back in Paris, he pushes it even further. Terracotta skin. Huge limbs. Those heavy faces. He'd been looking at Iberian sculpture. And you can feel it here. No elegance. No showing off. Just weight. Even the two women barely fit together. Like, can you scoot over? Nope. Now look at the woman on the left. She's pulling open a curtain. And she's looking right at you. Get a good look at her face. Then go find Les Demoiselles d'Avignon. It's hanging in this same gallery, painted a few months later. Look at the woman on the far left. Same curtain. Same chiseled nose. Same dark, hollow eyes. Picasso's not at Les Demoiselles yet. But she's already here.
