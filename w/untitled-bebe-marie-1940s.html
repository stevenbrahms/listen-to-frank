Untitled (Bébé Marie)

Joseph Cornell, 1940s · Gallery 522 · Floor 5

Find her eyes. There’s a gap in the twigs where you can see both. Big, dark, glassy. Looking straight at you. Straw hat with flowers on it. Somebody dressed her up. Before Joseph Cornell put her in here, she belonged to his cousin Ethel. Just her doll. Cornell lives in Queens with his mother and his brother Robert, who uses a wheelchair. Their street is called Utopia Parkway. Seriously, that’s what it’s called. He never went to art school. But he goes through Manhattan’s junk shops, and the dime stores back home, picking up things that might come in handy. Thimbles. Corks. Bits of old paper. Birds’ nests. Twigs. He brings it all home to the basement. Shoeboxes painted white, labels in blue. Old cookie tins full of stuff. Called it his spare parts department. Everything can be used, he said. You just don’t know it at the time. And he starts putting these things together in little boxes. Whole places you can look into, usually through glass. Before this box, he works on a close-up photograph of the doll. Right on the print, he inks lashes around her eyes. Then look at what he puts between you and her. All those twigs. Flecked with paint, like frost. She looks like she wandered into the woods in January wearing her flower hat. Not dressed for this at all. You can see her. You can’t get to her. After Cornell dies, his family and the people handling his estate get into a legal fight over his work. Who gets it. One of the things they argue over is who owns Ethel’s doll.
