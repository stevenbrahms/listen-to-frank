Colombia

Colombia by Antonio Caro, 2010 (after 1976 original), is in Gallery 412 on Floor 4 at MoMA. Frank's free, unofficial audio guide tells its story in 1 min 32 sec.

Antonio Caro, 2010 (after 1976 original) · Gallery 412 · Floor 4

Alright. Look. It’s just a word. Red. White script. The font you’ve seen before, on a can, in every deli from here to Bogotá. Your brain’s already finishing the word. Co, co… You know how this goes. Then your eye catches up. Oh. Wait. The guy is Antonio Caro. He’d done a little time in advertising. And he once showed up at the national salon with a salt bust of the president and dissolved it with water at the opening. Flooded the gallery. Then he tried a logo. Marlboro. A jury turned it down. Later, at a party, a woman says to him, you already did the Marlboro, why not do the Coca-Cola? So he does. He said it was easy. Colombia and Coca-Cola have the same number of letters. Eight. Count them. Even the big C does the swoop. That handwriting belongs to an American soda company. Trademarked. Shh. Nobody tell Coke. A poet called it a charming bit of silliness. And then one of the jurors called it real political art. Caro used to say he was stupid. And clumsy with his hands. Doubly clumsy, he said. Got one idea a year, then kept at it until everybody was tired of it. This one, he kept at for the rest of his life. He said it's the one that made him an artist. Back home, it's a classic. Now, this one was made decades later. Looks like it came out of a factory. It didn't. Two guys painted it by hand, on steel. Swoop and all.
